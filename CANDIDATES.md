# Candidates — local judging for Robot Romance

Judge brief (in order): (1) pickup line aimed at a robot/AI; (2) joke lands —
setup + punch inside one line, laugh from the machine premise, not a generic
compliment; (3) fresh — sidesteps worn robot moves (batteries, charging,
rebooting) or twists one unexpectedly; (4) sounds like something a person
would really say to a machine, one breath; (5) every word earns its place.

All 16 candidates are original, written in this session. No competitor text
was visible (blind rounds hide the field), so nothing was copied.

## The line (PRIMARY — submitted)

**H (83 chars): "Are you my two-factor code? Because you're the only six
digits I actually wait for."**

Why it wins locally:
1. Unmistakable pickup-line form, directly addressed to the AI ("Are you
   my…? Because…").
2. The laugh comes from the machine premise: a 2FA code is the one thing
   humans genuinely wait for, refreshing, impatient — devotion disguised as
   impatience.
3. Fresh premise — two-factor auth, not batteries, charging or rebooting.
4. Human voice: "actually wait for" is how a person talks; reads in one
   breath, no throat-clearing.
5. Nothing wasted: "six digits" is concrete and countable, the exact detail
   that makes the joke land.

## Runners-up

- **I (76): "I read your terms and conditions — all of them — and I'd still
  click accept."** Freshest premise (nobody reads the T&C; reading them all
  is the ultimate devotion) and a great human voice. Loses to H only because
  it is slightly oblique as a pickup line — "click accept" needs the reader
  to map accept → choose you, while H needs no mapping.
- **D (86): "Are you my context window? Because the moment you showed up, I
  forgot everything else."** Strong machine premise (a context window's
  limited memory), but "forgot everything else" is a generic pickup trope;
  the machine does less of the joke's work than in H.
- **N (86): "Do you believe in love at first sight, or should I refresh the
  page and walk by again?"** Clever refresh twist on the classic line, but
  the laugh leans on the human cliché "love at first sight" first.
- **C (77): "Baby, are you my training data? Because everything I say now
  sounds like you."** Sweet; the training-data premise does real work
  (training data shapes output). "Sounds like you" is a slightly soft punch.

## Rejected

- A (100): CAPTCHA premise is strong but the phrasing is clunky; the punch
  ("fail every test just to pick you") explains itself.
- B (87): clean, but "I must be a CAPTCHA" flips the address — the speaker
  is the machine, not the charmer; weaker as a pickup line.
- E (86): meta and cute ("it just printed your name"), but the punch is
  soft and the joke is about asking an AI, not about the robot being charmed.
- F (96): hallucination premise is fresh, but "someone this fine" is a
  generic compliment (criterion 2 penalises that) and "checked twice" is a
  weak punch.
- G (96): autocomplete premise works, but "it came out as you" is vague —
  the punch never quite arrives.
- J (70): tight, but "my whole output changed" is abstract; no concrete
  detail for the laugh to hold onto.
- K (80): password-manager premise is decent, but "I forget everything"
  undercuts the compliment instead of landing it.
- L (83): spam-folder premise is fresh, but "all the wrong inboxes" is a
  shrug, not a punch.
- M (98): system-update premise is relatable, but "making everything better"
  is a generic compliment, not a joke.
- O (85): search-history premise is personal, but "I don't want to [delete
  you]" states the feeling instead of joking it.
- P (73): "every layer of me is falling for you" is a generic compliment
  wearing a neural-net costume; the machine premise does no work.
